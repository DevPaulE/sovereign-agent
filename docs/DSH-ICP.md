## The question

**Who exactly is the solo operator this practice installs a dæmon for — and is "their own business agency, there whenever they want it, never taking over" a real, defensible position or a nice sentence?**

The job is to turn one family member's profile into a **repeatable archetype**, or to break the archetype and say so.

## The archetype (Paul's words — do not paraphrase them away)

> *"solo operators who live in the chaos and want their own business agency as they have been operating it; but the 'AI chatbot' is there whenever they want it."*

Unpack that, because each clause is a constraint:

- **"solo operators who live in the chaos"** — they run their business by feel, not by pipeline. They **resist being systematized**. They are not asking to be organized.
- **"their own business agency"** — the agent acts as the back-office **they never had**, but it is *theirs*, and it serves *their* way of operating. Ownership is part of the ask.
- **"as they have been operating it"** — the tool adapts to the operator; the operator never has to change their workflow to use it.
- **"there whenever they want it"** — **on-demand, not always-on.** No ambient automation running at them. Pull, not push.
- **"never taking over"** — the dæmon **proposes; the human approves.** This is the gate doctrine (propose, don't commit) applied to a one-man shop.

## What I already know (verify, correct, extend)

**The reference instance — "John Paul" (the only one with measured facts).**
A cousin, in San Jose, owns SVAutoPros — a one-man auto-detailing / PPF shop. Non-technical. Works from a **laptop** (Square + Gmail are his whole workstation). **Hates dashboards.** Sends every customer-facing message himself; a Telegram bot **only drafts**, he approves before anything sends. Runs a high-ticket, low-volume book: **~$2k average ticket, ~50–55 jobs/yr** (down from ~93), **135 quotes never converted to a deposit (~$220k, $100k of it PPF), 265 customers with paid work of whom only 49 ever returned (18%)**. He ignored a mass "come-back" email (360 sent, 57% opens, **zero sales**) and is wary of automation that replaces his personal touch. He is **skeptical of anything he calls "theory"** and only trusts concrete, boring proof in his own tools. He has **explicitly voiced the continuity fear**: *"what happens when I'm gone — there's no system to manage."* He rejected being set up to talk to 10 customers at once as "slow" — he prefers to book **one good job at a time**. The design rule Paul holds: **automation expands which leads/quotes he sees and can choose from; it never forces him into a different mode of work.**

**The anti-pattern we are differentiating against.**
The dominant SMB "AI" motion is a **replacement** motion: AI receptionist, autonomous booking, CRM that forces a pipeline (e.g. **Urable**, used by the benchmark shop Chicago Auto Pros). Those require the operator to change how they work and hand control to the system. Our archetype buys the opposite: **amplification with approval.**

**The job framework already in our canon (use it; don't invent another).**
*jAI strategy-before-tactics*: a coherent strategy names **outcome, WHO, WHAT, WHY/evidence, the offer's economic role, constraints, and the learning metric.** *Fihn's shoe-buyer test*: does this buyer **already spend real money** on the adjacent job? *(Hormozi:* a real budget line, not "they should care.")

**The warm reality.**
This ICP is reachable **through family and one degree out**, not through a broker. The broker / M&A-intermediary path (a separate, fully-built doc) is **channel #2, after one published case study** — not this.

## Research tasks

1. **Firmographics that survive contact.** Find the honest common denominators of solo/owner-only service operators running **high-ticket, low-volume, judgment-heavy** work (detailing/PPF, specialty trades, mobile services, consultancies-of-one, bespoke fabricators). Which NAICS/SIC codes, revenue bands, ticket sizes, and staff counts actually cluster? Cite trade or market data where it exists; say "no data" where it doesn't.
2. **The trigger.** What event makes one of these operators **seek** help? Age/succession, a health scare, an unbooked month, a rival who took their customers, a spouse asking "what happens if you're gone," or a bottleneck they'd pay to remove. Rank the triggers by how observable/actionable they are.
3. **The feared outcome and the language.** In *their own words* (forums, reviews, Reddit, trade groups), how do they describe the problem and their distrust of software? Collect **verbatim buyer language**. We must never say "AI agents," "agentic," "FDE," or "AI receptionist."
4. **Existing spend (the shoe-buyer test).** What do these operators already pay for monthly — Square, Wix, booking tools, call-answering, lead services, bookkeeping, EDDM/direct mail, Google Ads, a VA? Which line item does an on-demand agency **reprice or replace**? Evidence, not assumption.
5. **The differentiation test.** Compare **on-demand, approval-gated agents** (pull) vs **always-on autonomous "AI employees"** (push) for this buyer. Is "there whenever they want it, never taking over" a *real* wedge, or are buyers indifferent? Find launch/retention evidence either way; correct us if a major vendor already owns this positioning.
6. **The one deliverable.** For this archetype, is the first artifact **one recovered week**, a **gated weekly list + drafts they send** (which is what the reference shop's continuity design produced), or a **dossier of what only they can decide**? Pressure-test which single deliverable is legible, gateable, and sellable to *this* person.
7. **Watering holes and proof.** Where do these operators actually gather (subreddits, Facebook groups, trade associations, podcasts, conventions), and what form of proof moves them (a number in their own account, a peer's before/after, a video of the thing running)? Public sources only.

## Constraints

- **Public sources only.** No login-walled content. No unattended browsing of authenticated social/account sites.
- Distinguish **shipped** from **announced** from **speculated**; **date every claim.**
- For each finding, label it **adopt** (use in the ICP) / **avoid** (we're differentiating against it) / **watch**.
- Do **not** invent metrics. If a number isn't in a source, write "not sourced." The reference shop's numbers above come from his own Square (aggregate only) — treat them as evidence of *shape*, not as population statistics.

## Output

A markdown brief:

1. **The archetype, sharpened** — one paragraph, plus a firmographics table with sources and a "confidence" column.
2. **The trigger** — ranked, observable.
3. **Their language** — verbatim quotes with URLs, and the words we must never use.

7. **Watering holes + proof form** — where to meet them, what evidence converts.
8. **One recommendation** — the single most important thing to get right for this archetype, and the single most likely thing to be wrong. Say which of "solo operator / lives in chaos / own agency / on demand" is the load-bearing clause, and which is decoration.
