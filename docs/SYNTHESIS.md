## 1. The gate is the product (built)

The broker research found the **real buyer checklist** (`bigjoecoding/smb-acquisition-diligence`, 4 phases). We turned it into the **Dæmon Diligence Gate** (`GATE.md`): every line item a pass/fail test the dæmon's data room must survive. Demo = the **gap report**, not a full build. Gate-author ≠ builder — the checklist is external and can reject the dæmon.

## 2. The offer (the revenue bridge)

> **"Buyers discount what they can't verify — and after 30 years, most of your business lives in your head and your inbox. I install a dæmon that turns it into a diligence-ready data room in 30 days, for $2,500 — so a buyer prices the business you built, not the paperwork you didn't."**

The Exit-Readiness Pilot: one exit-bound owner, one broker, one gap report checked against the real checklist.

## 3. The opening (the competitor gap)

The market is fragmented and **none of it is yours**: VDRs (Ansarada, Firmex, iDeals) do *storage* but not production; consultants (fractional CFOs, brokers) do *advice* but not automation; OSS tools do *checklists* but not execution; all are cloud-only, mid-to-enterprise, no SMB tier. The report's own words: *"Nobody ties continuous owner-exit readiness → dæmon-produced data room → buyer's checklist verification into one owned package. That's the opening."* **Owned + gates + the dæmon + ongoing readiness** — the combination no one offers.

## 4. The positioning (the comms strategist)

A communications strategist *says things*; you're a **conversation engineer** — the system (sovereign-agent, fractal) + the human (Don Draper) + **the proof** (the data room, the gates). The report's line: *"A communications strategist tells your story. A conversation engineer builds the system that generates the evidence your story needs to be believed — and then tells it."*
