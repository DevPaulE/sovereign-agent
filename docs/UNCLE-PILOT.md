# UNCLE PILOT — the first junto member, end to end

> Family, in Manila. Problem: **scheduling + managing different tasks**. Today: ChatGPT/Grok. Owns a Tesla. This is junto member #1 — a **case study**, not a sales proof, and he does **not** skip the gate.

## His offer (one line, his sentence)

> *"I give you an agent that reads your calendar and your tasks and tells you what to do next — and you own it."*

## What he actually has (his words, not ours)

- A schedule he juggles across tools.
- Tasks that live in his head, in chats, in notes.
- ChatGPT/Grok sessions that **don't remember** and can't act on his real files.
- The pain: *nothing carries forward; every session starts over.*

That last line is the whole pitch — his current tools have no **Active Memory** and no **gate**.

## The app (what he touches)

A one-link web app on **exe.dev**:

1. **Upload** — he drops files (calendar export, a task list, notes, a photo of a whiteboard).
2. **Process** — the agent runs on the M6 (Paul's lab, for now) and reads them.
3. **Result** — a one-page **"what to do next"**: the tasks that matter, ordered, with anything time-sensitive flagged.

No terminal. No login beyond a link. He already "feeds AI stuff" — the app just gives the feeding a destination.

## The demo (the walkthrough, run live)

1. **His own files** → the app → a real plan. Not a sample.
2. **Your dæmon meets his.** Aria proposes a schedule; his dæmon proposes back. **The two of you approve.** The dæmons *negotiate*, the humans *decide* — the honest version, and the thing no single chatbot can do.
3. **The gate, shown, not told.** Every item in the plan cites the file it came from. He can check it.

## The gate (his version)

The same pattern as `check-ddg.py`, repurposed: the plan must cite his inputs. If it can't, it says so. **A family pilot still passes the gate** — that is the entire credibility of the case study.

## The honesty line (do not skip)

His files go to **Paul's M6** for now. Say it plainly: *"I'm running it on my machine while we build yours; when it's yours, it runs on your machine."* Never claim sovereignty while holding his data.

## What "done" looks like

1. His **case study** — before/after, in his words.
2. A **testimonial** — one paragraph, real.
3. A **repeatable app** — the same scaffold works for the next warm name.

## Build order

1. The app scaffold on exe.dev (upload → agent → one page).
2. The audit prompt (schedule/task map, gated).
3. The demo script (the two-dæmon proposal).
4. The settling (his tenant record: workspace, lane, state).
5. The move-to-his-box path.

**Success test:** the uncle says *"this remembers my stuff and tells me what's next"* — unprompted.
