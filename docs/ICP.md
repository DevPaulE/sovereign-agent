# ICP — the business broker, and the 40 dæmons behind him

> Expanded 2026-10-14. Sources: `Ideas/owner-independence-as-the-foundry-wedge` · Hormozi Scaling System (`Downloads/`, Oct 14 2026) · Fihn's AgencyBox shoe-buyer test (`AgencyBox/`) · BEN AI case study (`Downloads/A Realistic 60k/year AI Client Case Study.txt`) · IBBA (ibba.org).

## Who the buyer is

**The business broker / M&A intermediary** — main-street and lower-middle-market. Commission-paid (fee tied to enterprise value), carrying **10–40 live listings**, an IBBA member, using listing-management and data-room tooling already. Behind each broker sit **~40 exit-bound SMB owners in their 60s** who want to offload — the Everywhere Millionaires.

## Their pain (what the deal actually dies of)

1. **The key-person discount.** After an owner's death/departure, profit-per-worker falls **~82% over four years**. Buyers price that in. An owner-dependent business either doesn't sell or sells at a discount.
2. **The diligence mess.** Thirty years of records live in the owner's head, inbox, and shoebox. The data room is incomplete → buyer discount → price drop → deal dies.
3. **Listings that can't be marketed.** "The owner IS the business." Nobody else can run it, so no buyer can underwrite it. The broker carries a **dead listing** — and dead listings pay nothing.
4. **The rebuild tax.** Every listing gets the same diligence checklist, and the broker (or their analyst) reassembles it **by hand**, chasing the owner for financials, leases, contracts, SOPs.

## Are brokers shoe buyers? (Fihn's behavioural test)

**They are shoe buyers in deal tooling and data rooms — barefoot only in "AI."**

| They already spend on | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Data rooms & diligence platforms (DealRoom, Ansarada, Firmex) | per-listing SaaS |
| Valuation comps (BizComps, DealStats) | subscriptions |
| IBBA membership, CBI designation, conferences | annual + travel |
| Analysts/staff who assemble the data room | payroll |

**The rule this produces: sell into the flow they already have — the data room.** Never sell "AI." The offer is a **faster, cleaner, higher-priced data room**, and the dæmon is the engine behind it.

## Hormozi placement (stages 0–6)

- **Stage 1 (Monetize)** — fix the product until it's good enough to sell; ship V1; **get free clients → 10 testimonials → sell paid.** The Exit-Readiness Pilot is V1: one exit-bound owner, one data room, one broker checklist.
- **Stage 2 (Advertise)** — the **rule of 100**: 100 minutes/day of content (10 pieces + 10 minutes posting + 10 outreach). Cold content for brokers who don't know you; warm content for those who do.
- **Stage 5 (Productize)** — the Kiraa Engine/OS: the *same* harness installed for each SMB, configured per business. That's the productized version — after the pilot proves it.

## The content design (FAM + Meta + YT — "the algo will find them if you write for them")

Four pillars, written **for the broker**, never for the SMB owner:

| Pillar | The line |
|---|---|
| **The key-person discount** | "An owner-dependent business sells for less — or doesn't sell at all. Here's the number." |
| **The data room** | "After 30 years, the business lives in his inbox. Here's the checklist a buyer will run — and what's missing." |
| **The running concern** | "A system that keeps the business running when the owner steps out." (Never say dæmon to a broker.) |
| **The owned line** | "And the books never leave his own hardware." |

Channel: LinkedIn + YouTube for brokers (IBBA audience); Meta for the SMB owner **via the broker's** listing content.
