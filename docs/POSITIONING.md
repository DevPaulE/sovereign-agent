# Positioning — the conversation engineer, against the comms strategist

> Scott Galloway's *The $400k Skill AI Can't Replace* (youtu.be/Yp9KOqfnty0) prices the job; this document names the person who out-does it. **The comms strategist says things. The conversation engineer says things *and* proves them — with a system the client owns.**

## What a "communication strategist" actually is (the job Galloway prices)

Galloway's numbers (from the transcript, 2026):

- Storyteller roles: **50,000+** marketing + **20,000** media/comms postings (doubled 2024→2025).
- "Storytelling" on earnings calls: **147** (2015) → 359 (2024) → **469** (2025).
- OpenAI posted comms roles **above $400,000**; Anthropic tripled comms to ~80 people, some at **$400k** (vs **$106k** average director of comms). Netflix: a director of comms up to **$775,000**.
- OpenAI bought a 2-year-old livestream (TBPN) for a **low hundreds of millions** — *"they weren't buying a media company; they were buying a voice."*
- **74%** of new web pages have detectable AI content; **>50%** of new English articles are AI-written; ~1/3 of consumers avoid AI-ad brands.

The standard job description: narrative strategy, message architecture, executive communications, reputation and crisis, media relations, internal comms — reporting to the CMO/CEO. **Its KPI is share of voice, not change.** It can *say* the story. It cannot *prove* it, cannot *run* it, and its craft is now one prompt away for everyone.

## The conversation engineer (Paul, by principle)

**Two halves, one name:**

- **The engineer.** "My system" — `sovereign-agent` (the daemon, the archive, the gates, the onboarding). And it is **fractal**: the same pattern at every scale. He runs the conversation; his clients get their own engine; their clients get theirs. One kit, installed per person — the dæmon settles to them.
- **The conversation.** The human who makes people *feel* something — the Don Draper half. The experience is **human, and it is valued as human**: a real perspective delivered with craft (Galloway's own scarce thing). The Big Short instinct: see what the crowd misses and say it plainly.

**Why better than comms — the proof.**

| | Comms strategist | Conversation engineer |
|---|---|---|
| Words | sells the narrative | sells the narrative |
| **System** | none | the dæmon (owned, on-premise, gated) |
| **Proof** | testimonials at best | the **data room** a buyer can verify; the gates |
| **Delivery** | a deck | a working system the client owns |
| **Fractal** | one campaign | the engine, provided to client after client |

**The one-sentence difference:** *the comms strategist tells the buyer the business is worth more; the conversation engineer makes the business worth more — and hands the owner the engine that did it.*

## The proof the story couldn't carry

This is where the earlier judge's verdict pays: the dæmon promise failed the money test as a *sentence*. But as a **conversation-engineer** offering it passes — because the proof is not the words, it is the **Exit-Readiness data room** (a buyer-verifiable artifact, priced, delivered, owned). The story gets the meeting; the data room gets the price. That is what no comms strategist can produce: **narrative plus a verified, ownable system.**

## The positioning line

> **"I'm a conversation engineer. Most of your business lives in your head — I install a system that captures it, a dæmon that keeps it running when you step out, and a data room that proves what it's worth. You don't rent a strategist; you keep the engine."**
