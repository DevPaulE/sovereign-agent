# OUTREACH — the Brendan Dell framework, adapted to the dæmon

> Source: Brendan Dell, *The Freelance Formula* + *Client in Seven* (local corpus, `~/Documents/brendan-dell/transcripts/`, lessons `2171170632` · `2172363547` · `2187138185` · `2186577507`). Extracted 2026-10-15. Adapted to Paul's offer and the warm 25.

## 1. Dell's four rules of effective outreach

1. **Show me you know me.** The first line is the most important line — it decides whether they read on. Tell them *why you, now*.
2. **Peer to peer.** Write like an executive, not a salesperson. Concise, confident, consultative. **No commission breath.**
3. **Multi-channel.** An email touch *and* a LinkedIn DM. They won't switch channels on their own — say *"I sent you an email."*
4. **Watch your sender reputation.** 20–40/day **manually**. No "free / limited time" language. (Warm, human, one-to-one.)

## 2. The six email commandments

1. **The only goal is a reply.** Not a meeting, not a sale — a conversation.
2. **Personalize every single one.** Never spray and pray.
3. **Write at a fifth-grade level.** Easy to scan, quick to the point.
4. **30–150 words.** Executives scan to ignore; make it effortless.
5. **Subject line: 1–3 words**, title case, boring is best, descriptive, sounds *internal* (like it came from a colleague). **No verbs, no numbers, no questions.**
6. **Ask for interest, not a meeting.** *"Would you be open to…"* — easy to say yes to.

## 3. Dell's six-message / four-day sequence

- **Email 1 — the direct insight.** Show me you know me → the problem → **proof of result** → what you do → a low-pressure question.
- **LinkedIn DM.** *"I sent you an email"* + the note. (Puts a face on it.)
- **Email 2 — the power forward.** Forward Email 1, restate the problem, so it looks human.
- **Email 3 — a different issue, same theme.** A second angle on the same pain.
- **Email 4 — the value-added close-out.** *"Is this not the right time?"* + *"Can I send over how I help?"* It converts.

## 4. The no-nos (all statistically shown to *lower* response)

- ❌ Asking for a meeting on the first touch.
- ❌ **ROI calculations** ("clients typically see 5×…"). The skeptic brain reads it as made up — *you don't know me*.
- ❌ Guilting follow-ups (*"I haven't heard back"*).
- ❌ *"Thoughts?"*
- ❌ Multi-word, question, or marketing-smelling subject lines.

## 6. The five openers (ready to personalize)

> Replace `[slots]`. Keep each under ~120 words. Subject is 1–3 words, boring, internal.

### 1 — The owner-dependence angle (SMB owner)
**Subject:** `Operations question`

> Hi `[First name]` — `[one true thing: "saw you're still running [business] solo" / "you mentioned [X] last time we spoke"]`.
>
> Owners whose whole business lives in their head have a quiet problem: when they step out, so does the business.
>
> I build a small local system that reads a folder of your operations and hands back a one-page map — where you're the bottleneck, and the single task eating the most hours each week. It runs on my machine, not the cloud, so your data stays put.
>
> Would you be open to me sending you one, free? Five minutes to send the folder.

### 2 — The scheduling/tasks angle (the uncle's shape)
**Subject:** `Scheduling question`

> Hi `[First name]` — `[true thing about their juggling]`.
>
> You've got a calendar, a task list, and three chat threads that don't talk to each other — so nothing carries forward and every week starts from scratch.
>
> I build a small system that reads all of it and tells you what to do next, in order. It remembers. It runs on my machine, so your stuff doesn't feed anyone else's model.
>
> Would you be open to a look? I'd need one export and five minutes.

### 3 — The broker angle (dead listings)
**Subject:** `Listing question`

> Hi `[First name]` — `[true thing: "you had [N] listings up last I saw"]`.
>
> The deals that die usually die of the same thing: the owner *is* the business, and the data room is thirty years of files in his head.
>
> I build a small local system that reads a listing's files and returns a one-page gap map — what a buyer will discount, and what to fix first. Every line cites the file it came from.
>
> Would you be open to me running it on one listing, free?

### 4 — The job-change trigger (moved or promoted recently)
**Subject:** `New role question`

> Hi `[First name]` — congrats on `[the move / the new role]`. `[one true detail]`.
>
> New roles come with a pile nobody warns you about: the previous person's process, half in their head.
>
> I build a small system that reads the files they left and returns a one-page map of what actually matters and what to fix first.
>
> Would you be open to me sending you one?

### 5 — The peer / "I built a thing" angle (someone who'd enjoy the demo)
**Subject:** `Quick question`

> Hi `[First name]` — `[true thing about their work]`.
>
> I've been building something I think you'd get a kick out of: a small local agent that reads a folder and hands back a one-page map of where the bottlenecks are. It runs on my own machine — nothing goes to a cloud.
>
> No pitch. I want a real reaction from someone who'd tell me if it's useful.
>
> Would you be open to a look?

## 7. The follow-up (Dell's sequence, applied)

- **Day 1** — Email 1 + the LinkedIn DM (*"I sent you an email"*).
- **Day 3** — Email 2: **forward** Email 1, restate the problem. (Looks human.)
- **Day 5** — Email 3: a *different* issue (e.g., the key-person discount, or the hours a week).
- **Day 8** — Email 4: *"Is this not the right time? Can I send over how I help?"*

**Stop at four. Then wait 20 days and re-list.**

## 8. The one rule that beats all the others

**Send the message.** Dell's corpus says the same thing fh and jev said: *the more systems you introduce, the slower it goes.* Begin manually. The write-up is done; the sending is the price of freedom.
