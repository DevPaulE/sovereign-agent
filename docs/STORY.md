# STORY — the dæmon, through the Story Selling rail

> Brendan Dell's eight elements (audience · market message · product message, across three acts) applied to the sovereign agent. This is the **message architecture**, not the brochure. Source: Dell, *Story Selling* (`toolbelt/lifeos/knowledge-agentic/StorySelling/`). Companions: [`DAEMON.md`](DAEMON.md), [`../README.md`](../README.md).

Three-act inheritance: **Setup → Confrontation → Resolution.** A hero, a conflict, a resolution.

## Part 1 — Audience (Setup)

**1. Persona — the expert whose knowledge is the asset.**
The solo expert: consultant, coach, practitioner, owner-operator. Established, word-of-mouth, no leverage. Their expertise **is** the product — and it lives only in their head and their attention. They are the best-kept secret in their field.

**2. Company & industry.**
One-person or small team; no marketing habit; the **personal-AI space** — and specifically the *sovereign* side of it (own vs rent).

**3. Jobs to be done.**
*"Get what's in my head into something that works without me."* The thing within the thing: **not "an AI agent" — a second self that works while I sleep.**

## Part 2 — Market message (Confrontation)

**4. Problem — the status quo.**
The **rented agent.** Dots, ChatGPT, Muse: they know nothing about you, they own your data, they cannot be yours. Your expertise dies with your attention. You stay the best-kept secret.

**5. Stakes.**
If it isn't solved: your knowledge stays locked in your head; the rented products own your data and your business; your edge dies the moment you stop. **No stakes = a nice-to-have.** These are must-have stakes.

## Part 3 — Product message (Resolution)

**6. Promise — the one sentence they remember.**
> **You don't get an AI agent. You get your dæmon — and it settles to you.**

**7. Process — how the transformation happens.**
The **settling** (the onboarding interview) → the **four things** (front door · substrate · memory · conscience) → the **gates**. A proven process, not a feature list. You are the **dæmon-namer**.

**8. Benefit & results — why to believe you.**
The proof is a **demonstration, not a testimonial**: Paul's own dæmon — hemera on an always-on machine, the local LLM lab, the archive, the gates — built in the open. (Fihn's rule: *our buyers write our copy*.)

## The villain and the category

- **Villain:** the rented agent — the one that owns you.
- **Category:** a **dæmon** — a *buyable name* (a chair, not a "next-generation crouching support apparatus").
- **Market of one:** *"A single product for millions cannot settle; one dæmon per person can."* Naval's market-of-one, made literal.

## The principles applied

| Dell principle | Applied to the dæmon |
|---|---|
| "Most companies have a distribution problem, not a competency one" | The expert **is** the product; the dæmon is the distribution |
| "You do not want to compete. You want to be a market of one." | One dæmon per person is structurally a market of one |
| "Every yes is also a no" | Don't sell to everyone — sell the dæmon to the expert |
| "Start with them, not you" | The story opens on **their stuck knowledge**, not your tech |
| "No one is ever confused into buying" | One name, one promise: *your dæmon* |
| "We decide emotionally, justify rationally" | The dæmon (emotion) + the gates (rational proof) |
| "Stories are 22× more memorable than facts" | "Dæmon" beats "personal AI agent" on memory alone |

## The held-out test (a judge that rejects)

1. Would the expert **repeat "my dæmon" unprompted**?
2. Does it survive **"so what — how does this make me money?"** asked cold?

If it fails either, the promise is a brand, not an offer — and it goes back. *A judge that never rejects is not a judge.*
